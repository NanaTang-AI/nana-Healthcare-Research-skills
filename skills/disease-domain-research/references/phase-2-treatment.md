# Phase 2: Treatment And Clinical Management

Read this file only when the request includes treatment, clinical management, standard of care, treatment rate, response, or unmet need.

## Scope

Separate symptom relief, disease modification, organ rescue, complication prevention, and supportive/non-pharmacologic care. Organize treatment by the same patient-classification axes established in Phase 1.

## Evidence Priority

1. National guideline, national consensus, regulator label, and reimbursement list for the target country.
2. International guideline when national guidance is silent.
3. Systematic review/meta-analysis and pivotal randomized trial.
4. Large registry or real-world cohort.
5. Small cohort, case series, or expert opinion.

Do not treat recommendation strength as efficacy magnitude. Record evidence level and recommendation grade when the guideline supplies them.

## Treatment Pathway

Build a decision pathway:

`new diagnosis → severity/activity and organ-risk assessment → local/supportive care versus systemic therapy → first-line choice → response assessment → escalation/switch → long-term monitoring`.

For each branch state:

- target patient and clinical objective;
- regimen/class and regulatory status in the requested geography;
- expected benefit and time to response;
- key safety/monitoring requirements;
- response or failure criteria;
- next-line option;
- access/reimbursement constraint.

Distinguish off-label use, guideline-recommended use, local approval, and investigational use. A commonly used therapy is not necessarily approved for the disease.

## Treatment-Rate Data

Define the numerator before reporting a treatment rate:

- any symptom-relief treatment;
- any prescription drug;
- systemic immunomodulatory treatment;
- guideline-concordant treatment;
- active treatment during a defined look-back window;
- ever-treated.

Do not combine these rates. State care setting and data source because tertiary-center cohorts usually overrepresent severe and treated patients.

## Unmet Need

Map each unmet need to an identifiable patient segment and consequence. Use evidence for diagnostic delay, non-response, relapse, organ damage, quality of life, treatment toxicity, access, or lack of approved disease-modifying therapy. Avoid generic “high unmet need” statements without supporting facts.
